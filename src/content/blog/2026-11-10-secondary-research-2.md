## Secondary research - 2 ## 

### Why a second round?
Our first round of desk research covered children from 6 to 12 and was pretty limited and sloppy. When we narrowed the audience to 9-12, a lot of it no longer fit, so we went through it again and kept only what was about this age group and useful for the installation.

### How we worked
We kept one or two sources per theme, preferably the ones closest to 9-12. Every figure was checked in the original report, with a page number. Where a source didn't fully fit (only adults, a small sample, answers from parents instead of kids), we noted that next to it.

### What we found
Gaming is their main thing. Flemish surveys (Apestaartjaren 2026, MediaNest 2025) show gaming is the top media activity, and 86% of 9-12-year-olds game. In the UK, gaming peaks at around 10-12. Minecraft and Roblox come back everywhere: with simple controls, + played together with friends.

- Watching pulls people in, but being watched can be pressuring. This is the honeypot effect: people stop, watch, learn the rules and join. In a study with kids aged 9-13, onlookers made them want to try, but some also felt stupid playing in front of others.

- Feeling capable keeps them playing. Self-Determination Theory says people keep playing when a game makes them feel capable, free and connected to others.

- Absurd humour is their language. Funny clips are what kids watch most on YouTube, and tweens like absurd, slapstick humour best. It's also social: they laugh at it together.

- Fair beats lucky. Kids aged 8-11 rejected unfair outcomes, even when a fair coin toss decided them.

### What it changed ### 
These findings fed straight into our design principles. We added "Fun to watch" because of the honeypot research, and made sure the style stays absurd without becoming babyish, because these kids see themselves as real gamers.

### Still open ### 
We found no good research on how 9-12-year-olds handle failing in front of friends, or which games specifically Flemish kids this age play most.
