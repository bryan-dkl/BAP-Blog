## Finalizing the concept: Gulzilla ##

### How we got here? ###
Early on we brainstormed a lot of game ideas and chose to go game-first, with film culture as a layer on top. One of those ideas was a giant monster that a group controls together. It went from the name Monster Run, and ended up as Gulzilla: a mix of the Dutch word "gulzig" (greedy) and Godzilla. With all the research, insights and principles in place, we could now pin the concept down.

### The Concept in short ###
Gulzilla is a rhythm game installation for 2 or 4 players, made for children aged 9-12. Together, the players control one monster that ravages throughout the city looking for food.

**USP**: together you are the monster. Each player controls a limb, and only in sync can you ravage your way through the city.

### What, How and Why ###
We used the golden circle to explain the concept from the inside out.

- Why: kids have the most fun when they can work and win together, and when everyone counts, no matter their age or size.
- How: each player controls a limb as input, and the team has to work together rhythmically (with 2 or 4 players).
- What: an audiovisual rhythm game installation for JEF, for 2 or 4 players, mainly for children aged 9-12.

### The Monster ###
Gulzilla is a 30-metre green lizard. In love with snacks, takes really long naps in the sea, accidentally wipes out cities (sometimes) and is really clumsy. We wanted a monster that's funny and a bit silly instead of scary, so it fits "Goofy, but not childish".

### The Setup ###
The installation has one screen and four inputs: two upright pads for the hands and two pads on the floor for the feet. Each input has its own food. Waffle and donut are the hands, pickle and pizza are the feet. The food notes come down on screen and the players hit their pad on the right time.

### Link with Film Culture ###
The link with film culture comes in two parts: film and music.

- **Film**. The concept builds on giant-monster causing chaos in the city, films like Godzilla, King Kong and Pacific Rim. Pacific Rim is especially close: there, two pilots have to move in sync to control one giant robot. In Gulzilla, the whole group shares one body in the same way.

- **Music**. Because it's a rhythm game, the music drives the whole game. The notes follow the beat, so sound tells the players what to do, and doesn't just sit in the background. Like a score in a film, the music also sets the mood and builds up towards each checkpoint.
