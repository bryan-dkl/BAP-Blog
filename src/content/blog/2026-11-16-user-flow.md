## User flow ##

### Why a user flow? ###
With the context of use in mind, we mapped every step a group goes through, from walking up to the installation to walking away. We drew it as a user flow because there are several points where the path splits, depending on what the players do.

- Getting started
The installation waits on an idle screen with "press to start". The players then choose whether they play with 2 or 4. After that, each player activates their own station on their pad. This way we know everyone is ready and in the right spot before anything starts.

- Story and tutorial
A story cutscene of 15 sec. at most sets the scene. Then the tutorial tests each of the four inputs. It loops until the input is matched, so nobody can skip ahead without understanding what their pad does. This replaces any written instructions, since nobody at the festival will read them.

- Playing
A countdown of 3, 2, 1 starts the level. During gameplay, the group passes four checkpoints. At each one, a short cutscene of 10 sec. plays, and after each checkpoint the game gets harder. That gives the round a clear build-up and short breaks. At the end of the level the end cutscene plays.

- Failing and replaying
After 6 missed hits the game is over. The group can choose to try again, which sends them straight back to the countdown, without going through the story and tutorial again. After finishing the level, the group can choose to play again. If they say yes, they go back to choosing the number of players. If they say no, the installation returns to the idle screen for the next group.

- Timeouts
At choices, the game goes back to the idle screen after 10 seconds without input. Groups at the festival often walk away halfway, and this way the next group never finds a half finished game.


The flow helped us keep the time per group short and the cutscenes capped, and made sure there's no dead end where the game gets stuck.
