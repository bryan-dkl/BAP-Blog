## Defining the Target Audience ##

### Starting point ###
The JEF brief gave us a wide audience, children 6 to 12. It also says we can narrow that down. So we decided to do that, because a 6-year-old and a 12-year-old are very different players, or just in general people.

### Why 9-12? ###
Our research showed a clear shift around 9. Friendships become more important, kids get better at seeing things from someone else's point of view, and gaming becomes their main media activity. In Flanders, 86% of 9-12-year-olds play games, more than any other age group. That fits our concept well: a co-op rhythm game where timing and teamwork matter needs players who already know how games work and want to play together.

Younger kids would need more help to understand and keep up. Designing for both groups at once would mean making the game either too hard for the youngest or too easy for the oldest.

### Too narrow? ###
At one point we considered narrowing it even further to 9-10, because we were worried the art style might look too childish for 12-year-olds. In the end we kept 9-12. We realized we could solve that through the tone of the game, so the age range could stay. That's where the principles "Goofy, but not childish" and "Feels like a real game" come from.

### Who exactly ###
Primary audience: children aged 9-12 who visit JEF with their family or school. The game is built for 2 or 4 players.

Secondary audience: parents or older siblings who want to join in. The game should work for them too, without taking over. That's why timing counts and not strength: a 9-year-old contributes just as much as their dad.
